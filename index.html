Card fees make small payments uneconomical.

People like and share posts all day, and none of it pays whoever made them. Card networks charge a fixed fee on every transaction, and on a payment of a few cents that fee can be bigger than the payment itself. That is why paying for things online still means a subscription or a checkout page. PennyPay is built for the amounts underneath that, from 1¢ to $3.

The architecture behind each tip

View the patents ↓
Card economics
Card networks charge a fixed fee per transaction. That makes payments under a few dollars too expensive to process one by one.
Pre-funded balances
Readers fund a balance once. Each tip is a ledger entry between hosted wallets, not a new card charge.
Publisher integration
Publishers embed the tip button next to their content and earn a configurable share of every tip.
Settlement rules
Balances settle on a schedule or once they cross a threshold. Each party’s share is set by rule.

A paid button next to the like button.

A low-fee micropayment system that lets users tip 1¢ to $3 on any article, video, or post.

Patented ledger technology designed to settle sub-dollar transactions without losing profits to credit card processing fees.

PennyPay holds two issued U.S. patents with an October 28, 2018 priority date. They cover how tips on third-party content move through hosted wallets and settle automatically among every party.

United States Patent · Original

US 11,875,330 B2

Systems and methods for a micropayment solution for media applications

Covers tips of $0.01 to $3.00 sent from a button beside a creator’s content. A central platform hosts consumer and creator wallets and moves funds between them for users. Balances settle automatically, split by rule between creator, website host, and platform.
