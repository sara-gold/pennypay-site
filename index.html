Card fees make small payments uneconomical.

Audiences like, share, and comment on content all day, and none of it pays the people who made it. Card networks charge a fixed fee on every transaction, which can exceed the payment itself when the amount is a few cents. So paid interactions online stay large: subscriptions, donations, purchases. PennyPay handles everything below that line, from 1¢ to $3.

Technology

The architecture behind each tip

Patents ↓
  1. 1

    Card economics

    A fixed card fee on every transaction makes payments under a few dollars too expensive to process one at a time.

  2. 2

    Pre-funded wallets

    Users fund a PennyPay wallet once, then tip in context on any participating site or app.

  3. 3

    Creator payouts

    Creators collect tips across every site they publish on and receive payouts after platform and service fees.

  4. 4

    Publisher integration

    Publishers embed the tip button next to their content and earn a configurable share of every tip.

  5. 5

    Transaction layer

    The patented architecture links each user action to hosted wallets: tip intent, payment initiation, wallet updates, transaction records, and analytics.

    user intentpayment initiationwallet updatesrecordsanalytics
  6. 6

    Settlement

    Balances settle on a schedule or at a balance threshold and are distributed to each party by rule.

Patents

PennyPay’s core asset: protected platform architecture.

Two issued U.S. patents, both with an October 28, 2018 priority date, cover how PennyPay connects tips on third-party content to platform-hosted wallets and automated settlement between consumers, creators, websites, and processing services.
